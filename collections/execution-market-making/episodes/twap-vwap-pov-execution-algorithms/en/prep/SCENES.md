## Slide 6: Capturing Intraday Spikes Without Skewing the Curve. 434.2 to 529.9 (95.7 s)

**Content**
- Title "Capturing Intraday Spikes Without Skewing the Curve".
- Text: "Perpetual futures funding timestamps create volume spikes, not shelves. Crypto volume is heavy-tailed—a single liquidation cascade shouldn't poison the curve forever." / "The statistical defense: use median over mean."
- Chart Volume vs Time: ochre "Mean" line with huge spikes, drifting upward; teal "Median" smooth line.
- Code window "Instructional Python Snippet":
  ```python
  import pandas as pd
  def volume_curve(trades: pd.DataFrame, bucket='30min') -> pd.Series:
      v = trades['qty'].resample(bucket).sum()
      day_total = v.groupby(v.index.date).transform('sum')
      share = v / day_total
      key = [v.index.dayofweek, v.index.time]
      # median, not mean: defend against liquidation cascades
      curve = share.groupby(key).median()
      return curve / curve.groupby(level=0).transform('sum')
  ```
  (`curve = ...median()` line highlighted.)

## Slide 4: The Endogenous Signal Bet of POV. 529.9 to 631.9 (102.0 s)

**Content**
- Title "The Endogenous Signal Bet of POV"; "POV abandons volume forecasting to trade a fixed fraction of observed volume."
- Big crimson formula `x = (γ / (1 − γ)) * M` with callouts: x "Your execution quantity.", γ "Your target participation rate.", M "Volume traded by the rest of the market."
- Teal-topped box "**The Catch: Endogenous Divergence** — The signal is endogenous. At a 0.5 participation rate, you are matching the entire market one-for-one, and the fixed point diverges to infinity. POV also structurally drops the completion guarantee."

**NotebookLM error (flag, also in the narration at 614.5 to 626.2):** at γ = 0.5, γ/(1 − γ) = 1, so x = M: finite, one-for-one with everyone else; nothing diverges. The fixed point x = γ(M + x) ⇒ x = γ/(1 − γ)·M only diverges as γ → 1. (The article's own sentence "at γ = 0.5 you match the entire rest of the market one-for-one and the fixed point diverges" carries the same slip; "one-for-one" is the correct part.) Redraw as a plot of γ/(1 − γ) vs γ with marks 0.10 → 0.111 (11.1% of others' volume), 0.25 → 0.333 (33%), 0.50 → 1.0 (one-for-one), vertical asymptote at γ = 1. Do not put "diverges to infinity at 50%" on screen. Also 1150.5 ("keep the participation rate well under 15% to avoid the mathematical divergence"): the ≤15% rule in the article is about footprint, gaming and cascades, not divergence.
