# Scene guide: complex-arbitrage-graph-algorithms (en)

This is the cartoon episode for "Complex Arbitrage in Rust", part 1: 23 min, 1920x1080, 30 fps. Everything is drawn in code on one canvas per scene (HyperFrames + the Arbitrage Crab kit). No image or video generation is used.
Read these first:
- `../../../../style/STYLE.md` is the style bible.
- `../../../../style/kit/README.md` lists the kit.
- `../prep/SCENES.md` is the content and the beats. It is the source of truth for every number, formula and code line.

## 1. How the episode is built

```
episode/
  scenes.json          ordered, contiguous scene list: id, slide, start, end (episode-global s), out transition
  data/sync.json       narration sync (tools/build_sync.py): loudness envelope, speaker per frame, words
  lib/                 shared episode code, appended after the kit:
    60_narration.js    NR: cue(word) -> time, talk(g, who), accent(g, who), env, who
    65_host.js         HOST: crabPose, parrotPose, duo (raft stage with both hosts)
    66_signs.js        SIGN: chapter (section title board), note (callout), stamp (RISK-FREE etc.)
    67_side.js         SIDE: horizon set with pan / zoom / storm / murk; HOST.ship (ship with both hosts)
    68_transitions.js  TR: wave and iris halves at scene boundaries (automatic)
    69_runtime.js      RUN(draw): timeline, transitions, error-to-canvas, grain, debug strip
  scenes/<id>/src/*.js the scene (one file is enough); _template/ is the starting point
  tools/               build_sync.py, build_scene.py, snap.sh, render_scene.sh, new_scene.sh, assemble.py
  renders/             (git-ignored) scenes/<id>.mp4, preview_en.mp4, episode_en.mp4
```

- One scene is one renderable HyperFrames project, `scenes/<id>/index.html`. It is generated by `tools/build_scene.py` from the kit, the generated `SCENE` / `SYNC` header, `lib/` and `src/`. Never edit `index.html`.
- Scenes are rendered silent. `tools/assemble.py` concatenates them, muxes the original NotebookLM audio, and puts the intro in front with a wave wipe.
- **Time is episode-global.** `draw(g)` gets `g` = audio seconds, the same numbers as SCENES.md and subtitles. `K.now` is also `g`, so the boil and the waves continue seamlessly across scene cuts.

## 2. Make a scene

```bash
cd episode
tools/new_scene.sh s05_complexity          # id must be in scenes.json; copies scenes/_template/src
# edit scenes/s05_complexity/src/50_scene.js
tools/snap.sh s05 2.5                      # debug build + a frame every 2.5 s -> scenes/<id>/snapshots/sheet.jpg
tools/snap.sh s05 "601.2,606,612.5"        # or exact GLOBAL times
tools/render_scene.sh s05_complexity       # silent render -> renders/scenes/<id>.mp4 (retries 3x)
python3 tools/assemble.py --preview        # intro + every rendered scene from the start, with audio -> renders/preview_en.mp4
```

A scene file has four parts. Copy the template:

1. `TXT`: every string the viewer reads, copied exactly from SCENES.md, with its corrections applied.
2. `B`: the beats, taken from words: `NR.cue("vertices", 197)` returns the global start time of the first word "vertices" at or after 197 s. Pass `after` about 1 s before the SCENES.md time. Use the transcript spelling (see the quirks in SCENES.md: "Belmond Ford", "PetGraph", "VEC deck"). `NR.cueEnd` gives the word end. Never hard-code a guessed second when there is a word to hang it on.
3. Camera: `K.mkTrack` keys on beats, either chart set (`K.applyCam` + `K.chartSea`) or side set (`SIDE.draw`).
4. `draw(g)`: the world, then the explainer objects, then the hosts in screen space. End the file with `RUN(draw)`.

Helpers from the runtime: `pop(g, g0, d=0.4)` gives 0 to 1 from g0, `span(g, g0, g1)` gives 0 to 1 over a range, and `hold(g, g0, g1, fade=0.3)` gives 0, then 1, then 0.

## 3. The hosts (the crab tells the story)

- **Captain Ferrous (crab) = host A**, the lower voice (about 102 Hz). He is the expert who explains. **Pip (parrot) = host B**, the higher voice (about 179 Hz), the curious first mate. The speaker labels come from pitch clustering and are about 95 % right. Short "Right." / "Exactly." interjections are sometimes swapped, which is harmless.
- Mouths, gestures (claw raises on stressed words), listening looks and nods all come automatically from the sync data. Your job is the **acting on top**, through `reacts` / `preacts` arrays: `{ at: B.x, kind, dur }`.
  - Crab kinds: `surprise`, `grin`, `worry`, `shrug`, `thumbs`, `jump`, `spyglass`, `nod`, `think`.
  - Parrot kinds: `surprise`, `flap`, `worry`, `grin`, `think`.
- Pointing: `point: [sx, sy]` (a screen point; use `K.toScreen(x, y)` in the chart set) plus `pointAmt: hold(g, a, b)`. Point at the thing being explained when it appears.
- Placement:
  - `HOST.duo(ctx, g, { x, y, s })` draws a raft in screen space. Default crab feet are (300, 1010), s 1.15, with the parrot to the right. Use `side: -1` with x about 1620 to put the duo bottom-right.
  - `HOST.ship(ctx, g, {...})` is for side-set scenes.
  - Keep the hosts in frame almost always. They can step back (`a` fades the duo), but a host reacting to every reveal is the point of the series.
- At least one deliberate reaction every 10 to 15 s: surprise on discoveries, worry on caveats, a thumbs-up on results, think on questions.

## 4. Layout, type, pacing (hard rules)

- **Safe area**: keep 64 px from the frame edges. The hosts own the bottom-left corner (about x < 640, y > 700 with `duo`) or the bottom-right one with `side: -1`. Explainer objects go in the rest of the frame. At most 2 explainer objects in focus at once.
- **Minimum text size: 28 px body on screen** (world size times camera zoom). Titles are 44 to 62 px, code 28 to 32 px, formulas 48 px or more. Text sits on paper, a slate or wood, never on raw sea. Text never boils (`K.text`).
- **Show everything in SCENES.md**: every number, formula, code line, table cell and caveat, legibly, and **on screen for at least `max(3 s, words / 3 s)`**. A formula is written on the slate in time with the words that read it aloud. Code is typed into the logbook, and the line under discussion is highlighted.
- **Pacing**: something meaningful changes every 3 to 6 s (a reveal, a camera move, a host action, a highlight). The camera moves through the world. No static slides. Camera moves land on beats, and push-ins are at most 1.2x over 4 s or more.
- **Slide starts**: `SIGN.chapter(ctx, g, { at, until, lines: [title, subtitle, tagline] })` on the heading cue ("H2 1.1 ..."). Hold it 4 to 6 s.
- Gold is only for money and the winning path. Red (`P.loss`) is for caveats and losses.
- Transitions: set `out` in scenes.json (`wave` between slides, `iris` for "looking closer", `cut` inside one slide). The runtime draws both halves. Keep important content out of the first and last 0.6 s of a scene that has a wave or iris.
- Determinism: no `Math.random`, `Date.now` or `performance.now`, and no state carried between frames. Use `K.hash` / `K.mulberry`.

## 5. Kit primitives worth knowing

`K.node` (island + coin), `K.edge` (route; `arrow`, `label`, `lu`, `state: hot|plain|dim`), `K.edgePoint` (ship position and heading along a route), `K.tag`, `K.plank`, `K.ledger`, `K.slate` (`write` 0..1), `K.sailChart`, `K.notice`, `K.logbook` (`typed` 0..1), `K.banner`, `K.miniShip`, `K.coin`, `K.goldBit`, `K.coinPile`, `K.sparkle`, `K.glow`, `K.cog`, `K.crab`, `K.parrot`, `K.perch`, plus the episode helpers `SIGN.note` (callout with a leader arrow), `SIGN.stamp` and `SIGN.chapter`. If you need a new reusable primitive, add it to the kit (`style/kit/`) or to `lib/`. Rebuild `style/gallery` to check for regressions, and say so in your report.

## 6. QA loop (at least 3 rounds per scene, logged in REVIEW.md)

1. `tools/snap.sh <id> 2.5` and open `snapshots/sheet.jpg`, then the full-size frames at the key beats. The debug strip shows `g`, the speaker, the loudness and the current word, so check that each reveal lands on its word.
2. Check: readability (28 px or more, contrast, no overlaps or clipping), faithful content (compare with SCENES.md and `../prep/slides/slide_NNN.png`), crab acting that matches the speech, something changing every 3 to 6 s, and no empty or dead frames.
3. Fix, and snapshot again. Then `tools/render_scene.sh <id>` and watch the mp4 with the audio (`python3 tools/assemble.py --preview` or the scene range from `--only <id>`).
4. Log the concrete defects you found and fixed in `REVIEW.md` under the scene id.

Renders are heavy and other agents render in parallel. If a render fails from load, `render_scene.sh` waits 60 s and retries. Use `WORKERS=2` when the machine is busy.
